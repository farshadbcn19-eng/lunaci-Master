## 1. Purpose & Position in the Hierarchy

LDO002 answers *how* a single piece of content should be built. LDO003 answers *what must physically exist* — the photography, film, and portraiture — before that content can be built at all. Neither answers *what gets scheduled this week, on which platform, and why*. That is this document's job.

```
Brand Constitution
        ↓
Appendix A–C
        ↓
LMB001 / LMB002 / LMB003
        ↓
LDO000 / LDO001
        ↓
LDO003   (Visual Asset Production Plan — what must be produced first)
        ↓
LDO004   (THIS DOCUMENT — what, when, where, and for what purpose)
        ↓
LDO002   (Content Translation Playbook — how each piece is built)
```

In practice: LDO003 determines the shoot calendar a phase ahead. LDO004 schedules from what LDO003 produced. LDO002 shapes each individual piece as it is built. LDO001 gates it before it ships.

## 2. Scope & Constraints

This plan governs a **six-month horizon** from activation, structured so that its Global Principles (Section 3) remain valid as LUNACI expands into new markets, while its Activation section (Section 4) is explicitly a single-market instance that gets forked, not rewritten, for each new country.

Three constraints remain from the prior revision and still apply throughout:

- **No sales or conversion objective.** This content is asked to be seen, recognized, and remembered — not to sell, yet.
- **No paid media plan.** Organic content only; paid distribution is a separate future document.
- **Six months, then review.** Not a permanent structure — revisited in full at Month 6 via the Brand Audit Framework (Section 10).

## 3. Platform Strategy — Global Principles

*This section is market-agnostic by design. It should still be true in a market LUNACI has not entered yet. Only Section 4 (Spain Activation) changes when LUNACI expands to a new country — this section does not get rewritten per market.*

Four platform categories, defined by the role they play for a brand like LUNACI, independent of any single country's usage statistics.

### Visual-Discovery Platforms — Instagram & Pinterest
These are Tier 1 globally, structurally, for a Mediterranean luxury beauty brand — not because of any single market's current numbers, but because of what the platforms are built to do. Both are search-and-save behaviors, not scroll-and-forget behaviors; both reward long content lifespan over a single day's reach; both serve an audience actively interested in design, ritual, and aesthetic — precisely LUNACI's audience. Pinterest specifically extends the life of Visual Asset Plan (LDO003) output at near-zero incremental production cost, since it repurposes existing editorial and product photography into a discovery and SEO asset rather than requiring new content.

**Global role:** Instagram carries the full four-Pillar rotation and remains the primary home for LUNA and campaign storytelling. Pinterest carries Pillar A (ritual imagery), Pillar B (material/texture detail), and product photography specifically — a slower-moving, boards-based presence, not a daily-cadence channel.

### Short-Form Video Platforms — TikTok and Reels-Native Content
Global luxury beauty has shifted meaningfully toward short-form video as a discovery engine. This category is always relevant to plan for; how much weight it gets in a specific market is a maturity question, not a relevance question, and belongs in Section 4, not here.

**Global role:** Pillar D content, paced by local platform maturity, never abandoned entirely even where its current weight is low.

### Professional / Institutional Platforms — LinkedIn
LinkedIn's role for LUNACI is broader than governance and partner-facing content alone. It is where the brand can credibly narrate its own building process: leadership credibility, the philosophy behind Vaqar itself, product-development decisions, a point of view on the beauty industry, and — as LUNACI expands — its international presence market by market. This is still not a consumer sales channel, but it is a brand-building one in its own right, not merely an institutional footnote.

**Global role:** Pillar C content primarily, extended to Pillar B when the topic is genuinely about how or why a product was made, and to brand-level industry commentary that would feel out of place on a consumer channel.

### Emerging / Regional Platforms
Evaluated per market at the point of entry, never defaulted into or out of globally. A platform dominant in one market (for example, a regional messaging or discovery app) may be irrelevant elsewhere — this category exists so that decision is made deliberately per Activation section, not by omission.

## 4. Platform Strategy — Spain Activation (Year 1)

*This section is the current market instance of Section 3's principles. When LUNACI activates a second market, a new "[Country] Activation" section is added alongside this one — this section is not rewritten to accommodate it.*

### Instagram — Full Weight (Tier 1)
Spain-specific data shows Instagram at 89% daily usage overall, and — critically for LUNACI — especially strong among the 35–44 age group (92% daily usage), which sits closer to the brand's actual customer than TikTok's core audience. Instagram is also where Spanish users research before purchasing, not only where they are entertained. Reels engagement in Spain grew sharply against prior-year figures, meaning the format performs natively here.

**Spain role:** primary home for all four Pillars, full cadence per Section 7.

### Pinterest — Light, Deliberate Presence (Tier 2)
Not excluded, contrary to the prior revision. Spain's luxury and design-conscious audience overlaps meaningfully with Pinterest's use case, and the platform's SEO and long-tail discovery value compounds independently of daily posting effort. Activation starts light and repurposes Section 3's cost logic directly.

**Spain role:** 3–5 pins per week, built almost entirely from existing Editorial, Product, and Detail & Texture assets (LDO003 Categories 1, 2, 6) rather than newly commissioned content. Boards organized by Pillar and by product category. No dedicated shoot days required in Phase 1.

### TikTok — Secondary, Deliberately Paced (Tier 3)
TikTok reaches 76% daily usage in Spain and is growing, but skews younger (strongest at 18–34) and has not displaced Meta's dominance in this market the way it has elsewhere in luxury beauty globally. This is a pacing decision, not a dismissal — Section 3 keeps this category permanently relevant; Spain's current maturity keeps its Year 1 weight secondary.

**Spain role:** Pillar D content only, cadence per Section 7, reviewed at the end of Phase 2 for a possible weight increase in Phase 3.

### LinkedIn — Full Global Role, Spain-Paced Cadence
Spain does not change LinkedIn's role as defined in Section 3 — leadership credibility, philosophy, product-development narrative, and industry point of view all apply here as anywhere. What is Spain-specific is cadence, which starts modest and increases through the phases (Section 7), not the scope of what LinkedIn is allowed to say.

### Explicitly Out of Scope for Spain, Year 1
YouTube long-form and paid social remain excluded for this market and phase, per Section 2's constraints — not because Section 3 excludes them structurally, but because this Activation instance has not yet reached the point where either is warranted.

## 5. The Strategic Funnel — Reusing LUNACI's Own Progression

Most content strategies borrow a generic funnel (Awareness → Consideration → Conversion). LUNACI already has its own progression, documented in Appendix B, Constant 02, and it maps onto a content-planning funnel more precisely than a generic one — because it was written for this brand specifically.

**Visibility → Presence → Memory → Legacy**

This plan uses that exact progression as its strategic funnel. Each stage has a content emphasis and a dominant Pillar (see LDO002 Section 3 for full Pillar definitions):

| Stage | What it means for planning | Dominant Pillar(s) |
|---|---|---|
| **Visibility** | Content optimized to be seen for the first time by someone new | Pillar A, Pillar D |
| **Presence** | Repeated, recognizable exposure — the same voice and face, consistently | Pillar B, Pillar C |
| **Memory** | Content deliberately built to be recalled after the screen is off | Pillar C, best-performing repeats of A/B/D |
| **Legacy** | Not a content type — the compounding outcome of six months done consistently | All Pillars, evaluated together |

Legacy is not planned week to week. It is the thing this whole document exists to produce, and it is only visible at the Month 6 Brand Audit (Section 10).

## 6. Six-Month Roadmap

Three phases of two months each, each corresponding to one stage of the Visibility → Presence → Memory progression. Every phase below assumes LDO003's shoot-unit requirements for that phase have already been fulfilled — this calendar schedules from an existing asset library, it does not commission assets reactively.

### Phase 1 — Visibility (Months 1–2)
**Goal:** be seen, consistently, by the right audience for the first time.

- Instagram cadence ramps to full target rate (Section 7) by end of Month 1.
- Pinterest activates from Month 1 using existing Product and Editorial assets — no separate production required.
- TikTok begins as a soft-start: publishing consistently but without pressure to perform.
- LinkedIn: initial presence posts establishing the company exists and is serious — no more than 2–3 posts total across both months.
- Pillar weighting this phase: A and D lead, since both are built for first impressions and discovery.
- **Prerequisite check:** LDO003 Phase 1 production requirements (minimum 150 stills, 10 motion clips) must be met before Month 1 publishing ramps to full cadence.

### Phase 2 — Presence (Months 3–4)
**Goal:** the audience that has seen LUNACI once now recognizes it reliably.

- Pillar B increases in weighting — the Detail & Texture assets from LDO003 Phase 2 feed this directly.
- LUNA (Pillar C) becomes more prominent and continuous, not campaign-timed.
- LinkedIn cadence increases modestly to biweekly, now including Pillar B product-development narrative alongside Pillar C.
- Pinterest cadence may increase modestly if Phase 1 saves/traffic data (Section 8) justifies it.
- TikTok: review Phase 1 learnings; increase cadence only if native performance justifies it.

### Phase 3 — Memory (Months 5–6)
**Goal:** content that is recalled after the platform is closed — the actual test of Seen → Remembered.

- Deliberately repeat and refine the highest-performing formats from Phases 1–2, using LDO003's refinement shoot allocation, rather than introducing many new formats.
- Comments, saves, and shares are read qualitatively this phase (Section 8) as the most direct signal of memorability.
- Cross-platform coherence audit begins informally here and is formalized at Month 6 (Section 10).
- **End-of-phase check (Month 6):** the full Brand Audit Framework (Section 10) replaces the informal check used in the prior revision.

## 7. Weekly Cadence & Pillar Rotation

Baseline weekly cadence, steady across all six months unless a phase note above adjusts it.

| Platform | Format | Weekly Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| Instagram | Feed posts | 3–4 |
| Instagram | Stories | 4–5 (can cluster around a feed post) |
| Instagram | Reels | 2–3 |
| Pinterest | Pins | 3–5 |
| TikTok | Short-form video | 1–2 (Phase 1–2); reviewed in Phase 2 |
| LinkedIn | Posts | 1 (Phase 1); biweekly from Phase 2 |

### Pillar Rotation (applies across Instagram + TikTok combined, per week)

| Pillar | Phase 1 (Visibility) | Phase 2 (Presence) | Phase 3 (Memory) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — Vaqar in Daily Ritual | 35% | 25% | 25% |
| B — Mediterranean Origin & Material Honesty | 15% | 30% | 20% |
| C — LUNA, Not a Campaign Face | 20% | 25% | 30% |
| D — Presence in the Feed | 30% | 20% | 25% |

Pinterest and LinkedIn are not included in this rotation table — Pinterest draws from Pillars A/B by nature of its format (Section 4), and LinkedIn's low cadence is planned post-by-post rather than by weekly ratio.

This table is a planning guide, not a rigid quota — no Pillar should disappear for more than two consecutive weeks, and no single Pillar should exceed 40% in any given month.

## 8. KPI Framework (Branding-Only)

Every metric below measures whether LUNACI is being seen, recognized, and remembered — not whether it is being purchased. Section 9 states explicitly what is intentionally excluded.

### Visibility-stage signals
- **Reach & impressions** — is the content reaching new people, not just the existing audience.
- **Follower growth rate** — read as a signal of visibility, never itself the target.

### Presence-stage signals
- **Engagement rate** (likes + comments + shares ÷ reach) — repeated, active recognition.
- **Video completion rate / average watch time** — whether Reels and TikTok content holds attention long enough to register.
- **Comment sentiment quality** — sampled monthly by a human reviewer, read for tone and recognition rather than volume.

### Memory-stage signals — the most important tier
- **Save rate** — the single most direct proxy for Seen → Remembered, and the primary metric on Pinterest specifically, where saving is the platform's core behavior.
- **Share / send rate** — content passed on privately is a stronger memory signal than a public like.
- **Branded search volume growth** (Google Trends / Search Console for "LUNACI").
- **Direct / branded traffic share to the website** (vs. referral or ad traffic), including Pinterest-attributed referral traffic as a distinct line item given the platform's SEO role.

### Reporting Rhythm
Reviewed monthly per platform; reviewed together against phase goals at the end of each two-month phase; reviewed as a full body of work at the Month 6 Brand Audit (Section 10).

## 10. Brand Audit Framework

*The Month 6 review is not a general impression — it is five specific, named audits, each with its own method and pass criterion. Lighter versions of the first two may be run monthly; all five run in full at Month 6.*

### 1. Visual Consistency Audit
**Method:** sample the prior 60 days of published content across all active platforms; check against LMB002 §2.27 (Photography Constitution) and Appendix B's palette and typography constants.
**Pass criterion:** a viewer could correctly guess this is LUNACI content from the image alone, before reading any caption or seeing the logo, in the large majority of samples.

### 2. Tone Audit
**Method:** sample captions and copy against LDO002 Section 4 (Tone Matrix); flag any drift toward the "off-voice" column.
**Pass criterion:** zero Forbidden Language instances (Appendix C); Tone Matrix drift, if any, is isolated and explainable, not systemic.

### 3. Recognition Audit
**Method:** show 5–10 pieces of content, logo removed, to people unfamiliar with the brand's day-to-day production; ask whether they can identify it as LUNACI or describe its aesthetic consistently.
**Pass criterion:** a majority correctly identify or consistently describe the brand without prompting — this is the most direct test of whether Presence (Section 5) was actually achieved.

### 4. Community Audit
**Method:** read the accumulated comments and DMs from the full six months for recurring language, recurring misunderstandings, and sentiment patterns — qualitative, not a sentiment score alone.
**Pass criterion:** recurring audience language echoes Approved Brand Language (Appendix C) more than it echoes generic beauty-industry language; no recurring misunderstanding of what LUNACI is or does.

### 5. Search Audit
**Method:** review branded search volume trend and direct/branded traffic share trend (Section 8) across the full six months, not just the final month.
**Pass criterion:** a visible, sustained upward trend in unprompted brand-name search — the clearest external evidence that Memory (Section 5) occurred, independent of any single platform's algorithm.

### The Final Question, Applied at Scale
After all five audits, run LDO001's Final Question against the accumulated six months as a whole, not any single piece: *"If this were the only thing someone ever saw about LUNACI, would they remember the brand?"* If the honest answer across the five audits is not a clear yes, Phase 1 of the next six-month cycle should address the specific audit(s) that fell short — not simply repeat the prior plan.

## 11. Governing Authority & Precedence

*Effective under Revision 2.0, superseding Revision 1.0 (previously numbered LDO003). Horizon: six months from activation, reviewed in full at Month 6 via Section 10.*

LDO004 is subordinate to, and may never override:
- Brand Constitution
- Appendix A — Canonical Definitions & Brand Invariants (LMB003)
- Appendix B — Brand Constants (the Visibility → Presence → Memory → Legacy progression used in Section 5 is drawn directly from Constant 02 and may not be altered here)
- Appendix C — Approved & Forbidden Language
- LMB001, LMB002, LMB003
- LDO003 — Visual Asset Production Plan (governs what LDO004 is permitted to schedule)

LDO004 governs LDO002 in one direction only: it determines *what* gets produced and *when*; LDO002 determines *how* it is built once selected. LDO003 governs LDO004 in the same directional way: it determines what physically exists to be scheduled. Neither LDO002 nor LDO004 may be used to justify departing from Appendix B or C.

This document's Global Principles (Section 3) are written to remain valid across future market expansion without revision. Its Activation section (Section 4) is a single-market instance and should be duplicated, not rewritten, when a new market activates. The document as a whole is reviewed at the end of each two-month phase for tactical adjustment, and revisited in full no later than Month 6 via the Brand Audit Framework.
