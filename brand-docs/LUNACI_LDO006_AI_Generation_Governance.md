## 1. Purpose and Boundary

This document closes the gap between creative direction and machine production. LDO005 defines interpretive direction, LDO003 defines what must be produced, LDO004 defines what gets published, when, and where, and LDO002 defines how a single piece of content is written. LDO006 defines how those rules are translated into AI-generated images and motion assets — and, as of this revision, how that translation stays inside EU law.

It does not replace the Brand Constitution, Appendix B or C, the LMB series, the LUNA Character Bible, LDO005, LDO003, LDO004, LDO002, or LDO001. It sits beneath them and governs only the AI production workflow.

**A note on legal content.** Sections 5 and 6 summarize legal frameworks relevant to LUNACI's operations (the EU AI Act and the EU Cosmetics Regulation) as background for internal governance. Nothing in this document constitutes legal advice. Before this document governs live production, its legal sections should be reviewed by counsel qualified in Spanish/EU AI Act and cosmetics-advertising law.

## 3. The Five AI Governance Principles

**Principle 1 — Identity Fidelity before Realism**
The generated result must remain unmistakably LUNA, or unmistakably LUNACI when the asset is not a LUNA asset. A beautiful output that drifts from identity is a failure.

**Principle 2 — Reference before Prompt Flourish**
Approved references, registered examples, and governing documents outrank wording style. If a prompt is elegant but does not obey the reference set, it is wrong.

**Principle 3 — Versioning before Habit**
Prompts, workflows, model selections, and reference bundles are versioned assets. No AI workflow becomes permanent simply because it worked once.

**Principle 4 — Model Choice is a Governance Decision**
The model is not a neutral tool choice. It affects fidelity, texture, motion, drift, and repeatability — and, as of this revision, legal exposure. The selected model must be appropriate for the task, contractually compliant with provider-side transparency obligations (Section 5), and revalidated when the model changes.

**Principle 5 — Revalidation after Drift**
Whenever a model update, workflow change, or prompt revision alters output behavior, the approved reference set must be rerun and checked again before publication.

## 4. LUNA Transparency Policy

**Official policy:** LUNA is the official digital muse of LUNACI. She is an artistic digital brand muse and shall never be represented in a way that implies an undisclosed real human identity.

This statement serves two functions simultaneously:

- It is a **brand position**, consistent with LUNA's documented status throughout the LMB series and with the honesty standard the brand already holds itself to in Appendix C.
- It is the **baseline component** of LUNACI's compliance posture under EU AI Act Article 50(4) (Section 5). A public, standing declaration that LUNA is a digital muse is necessary but not, by itself, sufficient — Section 5 sets out what else is required per asset.

**What this policy does not do:** declaring LUNA's digital nature once, in a footer or About page, does not remove any individual asset from the legal tests in Section 5. Each qualifying asset still requires its own clear, human-perceivable disclosure at first exposure. The standing declaration and the per-asset disclosure work together; neither replaces the other.

**4.1 Confirmed Provenance of the Master Reference.** The canonical LUNA-Muse reference (Higgsfield element ID a1dc5075-cf86-4875-8ba5-85a7b7862df2, media ID a9f3d61b-8229-47f9-9102-435842d58bfa) is confirmed, per founder statement of record, to be **entirely AI-generated with no human source image** — not derived from, based on, or trained against any real individual's photograph. This is recorded here as the authoritative provenance statement for this asset.

*Effect on legal exposure:* this confirmation removes the GDPR/image-rights risk that would arise if the reference had been derived from an unconsented real photograph, and weakens the second prong of the Article 3(60) three-part test (Section 5.2) — the asset does not knowingly resemble a specific existing person. It does **not** remove LUNACI's disclosure obligation. LUNA still resembles a human being generally and would appear authentic to an ordinary viewer, which keeps the third prong of the test in play and keeps the Section 5 disclosure requirements active. Provenance confirmation reduces one category of risk; it does not substitute for disclosure.

## 5. Legal Compliance — EU AI Act, Article 50

*This section reflects the EU AI Act's transparency obligations as understood at the time of writing (August 2026) and LUNACI's internal interpretation of them. It is not a substitute for counsel review — see Section 1.*

### 5.1 LUNACI's Status
LUNACI is a **deployer** under the AI Act — an organization using AI systems in a professional capacity — not a provider of AI systems. Deployer obligations, not provider obligations, are what this section governs. Separately, LUNACI's AI vendors (the providers) carry their own machine-readable marking obligations under Article 50(2); Section 9 requires that vendor selection account for this.

### 5.2 The Three-Part Deepfake Test
An asset is a "deepfake" under Article 3(60), and therefore subject to the Article 50(4) disclosure obligation, when **all three** conditions hold:

1. The image, audio, or video is AI-generated or AI-manipulated.
2. It resembles an existing person, object, **place**, entity, or event.
3. It would appear authentic or truthful to a person, with a capacity to mislead.

**This test is asset-by-asset, not brand-wide.** A standing LUNA Transparency Policy (Section 4) does not exempt an individual asset from this test — it is part of how LUNACI satisfies the obligation once the test is met, not a reason the test doesn't apply.

**Scope beyond LUNA:** the "existing... place" language means AI-generated Barcelona location and atmosphere content (LDO003 Category 4) can also qualify, independent of whether LUNA appears in the frame. Location shoots are not automatically exempt from this section.

### 5.3 Disclosure Is Not Optional Once the Test Is Met
Where an asset meets the three-part test, **disclosure is a legal obligation**, not a best practice. What the regulation leaves to the deployer's discretion is *format and placement* — not *whether* to disclose. LUNACI's internal governance should never describe this obligation as optional.

Disclosure must be:
- **Clear and distinguishable** — perceivable by an ordinary person without special tools.
- **Delivered at first exposure**, at the latest.
- **Not solely dependent on platform-side automated labels.** Machine-readable marking embedded by an AI provider (Article 50(2)) does not, on its own, satisfy LUNACI's deployer-side disclosure obligation (Article 50(4)). LUNACI's own visible disclosure is required in addition to, not instead of, any platform AI-label feature.

### 5.4 Disclosure by Channel

| Platform | Required approach |
|---|---|
| Instagram | Short disclosure line in caption (e.g., "AI-created visual featuring LUNA, the official digital muse of LUNACI") **plus** the platform's native AI-content label where available. |
| TikTok | Platform AI label enabled **plus** the same short disclosure text in the description. |
| Website | Visible note beneath the asset or in the relevant page's footer, **plus** a standing "AI Transparency" policy page linked from it. |
| Press Kit | Dedicated "AI & Creative Transparency" section. |
| Media Kit | Formal explanation of LUNA's status as Digital Muse. |

### 5.5 A Narrower Exception Exists — Do Not Assume It Applies
Where content is part of an evidently artistic, creative, satirical, or fictional work, the disclosure obligation is *limited* to a manner that doesn't interfere with enjoying the work — it is not removed. Commercial cosmetics advertising is unlikely to qualify cleanly for this narrower treatment, and LUNACI should not rely on it without counsel confirmation. Section 4's framing of LUNA as an editorial, narrative presence (rather than a product-performance model, per Section 6) strengthens — but does not by itself establish — an argument for this exception.

### 5.6 Provider Vetting
Before any AI model or platform is adopted into production (LDO003, this document Section 9), it should be assessed for whether its provider meets Article 50(2) machine-readable marking obligations. This is added as a governance gate, not merely a technical preference.

## 6. Cosmetic Claims AI Policy — The Outcome Test

*Legal basis: Article 20 of Regulation (EC) 1223/2009 (no text, image, or sign may imply a characteristic or function the product does not have) and Regulation (EU) 655/2013's honesty criterion (before/after imagery must not be distorted by filters or digital retouching). Also relevant: Article 11's Product Information File requirement, which must contain evidence supporting any claim made.*

### 6.1 The Test
The determining question is **not** whether a product appears in frame, and **not** proximity between LUNA and a product. It is:

> **Does this image show a measurable, visible outcome — colour, coverage, or texture — resulting from the product's use on skin, lip, lash, brow, or nail?**

An image showing presence, gesture, or narrative connection to a product — without showing a colour or texture result — is a brand image, not a performance claim, and is not restricted by this section regardless of how close the product sits to LUNA in the frame.

### 6.2 The Three Tiers

**Tier 1 — AI Permitted Without Restriction**
Atmosphere, architecture, location, mood, light, closed packaging, and the texture of a product *not* applied to skin (e.g., product texture on a spatula, a swatch card, or a neutral surface — not on a body).

**Tier 2 — AI Permitted, Gesture and Presence Only**
LUNA or another figure holding a product, gesturing toward it, or standing near it — narrative and atmospheric use — provided **no visible colour or texture payoff is shown on skin, lip, lash, or nail**. A ring held near the face, a bottle in hand, a gesture toward a jar: all permitted under AI generation, because none of these depict a measurable outcome.

**Tier 3 — Photography Mandatory. AI Prohibited.**
Any image showing colour payoff, coverage, or a textural result of a LUNACI product on skin, lip, lash, brow, or nail. These must be photographed — the actual product, on an actual person, under normal conditions of use. No exception: an image that cannot be photographed under these terms is not published.

### 6.3 LUNA-Specific Rule
LUNA is rendered with natural, non-product-attributable styling only: no defined lip colour, no visible lash volume, no visible nail lacquer, and no complexion finish that could be read as the result of a specific LUNACI product. This keeps every LUNA asset inside Tier 1 or Tier 2 by construction, rather than requiring a judgment call on each individual image.

### 6.4 Reference Atlas Amendment
LUNA Character Bible V4.2, Module 11, entry **REF-20 ("Makeup Application — Intimate Touch of Lip Corner")** remains valid subject to one added constraint, recorded here and cross-referenced in the Character Bible: **no visible colour or texture payoff may appear on the lip in this shot.** The gesture is permitted; the result is not depicted. This is a condition, not a removal — REF-19 and other product-presence shots in the Atlas are unaffected, since holding a closed product shows no outcome and sits cleanly in Tier 2.

### 6.5 Product Information File Alignment
Every Tier 3 asset (photographed by definition) should have production records retained to support the Product Information File requirement under Article 11: SKU and batch, shoot date, model identity and signed release, number of product layers applied, whether any other product was used on the same area, RAW file retention, and the extent of any post-production. These records, not the AI Generation Record (Section 10), are the relevant compliance artifact for Tier 3 assets — Tier 3 assets do not carry an AI Generation Record at all, since no AI is used in producing them.

## 7. The AI Production Stack

Every AI asset is built through five layers. Skipping any layer creates inconsistency.

1. **Intent** — What is this asset for, and which Tier (Section 6) does it fall into?
2. **Reference** — Which approved images, canon entries, or examples define the target?
3. **Model Selection** — Which model is best suited to the task, and does its provider meet Section 5.6?
4. **Prompt Assembly** — Which versioned prompt, negative constraints, and reference instructions are used?
5. **Validation** — Does the output pass identity, mood, brand, and legal checks (Section 11)?

---

## 8. Approved AI Asset Classes

- **Master reference outputs** — canonical portraits, product frames, and approved motion stills used as anchors.
- **Exploratory outputs** — internal tests used only to compare model behavior and improve prompt control.
- **Approved production outputs** — assets cleared for use in LDO003, LDO004, LDO002, or LDO001 workflows.
- **Rejected outputs** — retained only when the failure teaches a boundary, in which case they are submitted to **LMB004 Register VI (Counter-Examples)** rather than held in a separate system; otherwise archived outside active use.

A file becomes brand memory only when it is explicitly admitted into the approved library or the Canon registry. Everything else is working material.

## 9. Governance Rules for Prompts, Models, and Outputs

- Prompt text may evolve, but every major prompt must have an ID, a version, a purpose, and a governing reference set.
- A prompt cannot override identity rules in the LUNA Character Bible, mood rules in LDO005, or the Tier classification in Section 6.
- A model cannot be used simply because it is newer or faster. It must be capable of preserving the approved visual language **and** meet the provider vetting standard in Section 5.6.
- Every approved output must be traceable to: the prompt version, the model version, the reference bundle, the reviewer, and — where Section 5 applies — the disclosure applied.
- When a model update changes texture, facial fidelity, motion continuity, or color behavior, all approved prompts using that model must be revalidated.
- Before any generation run, available AI credit/quota is confirmed — a credit failure discovered after generation wastes the reviewer step that follows it.

## 11. Technical Validation Gate

**Publication is prohibited unless all of the following pass:**

- [ ] Minimum resolution met (per LDO003: 1920px+ desktop hero, 1080px+ mobile hero)
- [ ] Colour space and aspect ratio compliant with delivery specification
- [ ] Provenance metadata attached to the file
- [ ] Approved workflow and vetted model used (Section 9)
- [ ] Canon compliance checked (LMB004)
- [ ] LDO005 compliance checked (mood, seasonal register, framing language)
- [ ] Tier classification (Section 6) confirmed and correct — Tier 3 content is verified as photographed, not generated
- [ ] Legal disclosure applied where the Section 5.2 test is met, per the Section 5.4 channel table

Any unchecked box stops publication. This gate sits alongside, not instead of, the LDO001 Approval Checklist.

## 12. Output Acceptance Criteria

- **For LUNA assets:** the output must preserve the approved identity architecture, expression discipline, wardrobe rules, and emotional register defined in the LUNA Character Bible, and must comply with the LUNA-Specific Rule in Section 6.3.
- **For LUNACI brand assets:** the output must remain compatible with the current phase mood, seasonal register, framing language, styling direction, and sound rhythm defined in LDO005.
- **For product assets:** the image must support the object-as-evidence standard rather than generic commercial decoration, and must be correctly classified under Section 6.2.
- **For motion assets:** the edit must obey the brand's pace, silence, and movement discipline; no motion may appear faster, louder, or more performative than the brand allows.
- **For all assets:** the result must remain recognizable without over-reliance on text, logo placement, or post-production explanation.

## 13. Drift, Supersession, and Revalidation

Model drift is expected. It is not a failure of governance; it is the reason governance exists.

When a new model version, system update, or prompt revision changes the output materially, the asset set must be re-run against the Canon and the accepted reference images before it re-enters active use.

An asset that no longer matches the approved look is not deleted from history, but it is removed from active rotation. A prompt that no longer produces the intended result is superseded, not silently reused.

---

## 14. Review Cadence

- **Monthly:** review active prompt versions, approved outputs, any newly rejected generations, and whether any model update or workflow change has altered fidelity.
- **Immediately following any major model update:** an out-of-cycle review is triggered regardless of the monthly schedule.
- **Every two months:** review the AI production stack together with LDO003, LDO004, LDO005, and LMB004.
- **Month 6:** run a formal governance review, including a legal compliance check against Sections 5 and 6, and retire any prompt, model choice, or reference bundle that no longer serves the brand.

Section 6 Tier questions require the second judgment explicitly, not by default inheritance from the first.
