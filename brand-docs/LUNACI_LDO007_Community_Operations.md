## 1. Purpose and Boundary

LDO004 governs what LUNACI publishes. This document governs what happens **after** publication — the comments, DMs, and replies a real audience generates once content is live. LDO002's Tone Matrix defines how LUNACI writes a caption; this document extends that same voice into a live, two-way conversation, where there is no drafting time and no second reviewer before the words go out.

It does not govern publishing decisions (LDO004), content construction (LDO002), or brand identity (Appendix B/C, LMB series). It governs the moment a real person writes something to LUNACI and someone has to answer.

**Current state:** Farshad Zamani is the sole owner of community response across all platforms. There is no team handoff yet, and this document is written to work correctly at that scale — and to scale cleanly when a second person is added.

**When a second person is added:** this section is updated to name who owns which platform. Until then, every rule below assumes one person is both the responder and, where needed, the escalation point.

**What this role actually does:**
- Monitors comments and DMs across Instagram, TikTok, Pinterest, and LinkedIn.
- Replies according to the tone and triage rules below.
- Recognizes when something is no longer a routine reply and moves it to LDO008.

## 4. Response Time Standards

Speed matters less than consistency. A brand that always answers within a day builds more trust than one that answers in ten minutes sometimes and not at all other times.

| Channel | Target response window |
|---|---|
| Instagram comments | Within 24 hours |
| Instagram DMs | Within 24 hours |
| TikTok comments | Within 24–48 hours (secondary platform, per LDO004 Section 4) |
| Pinterest comments | Within 48 hours (low-frequency platform by design) |
| LinkedIn comments | Within 48 hours |

**No one is expected to be online constantly.** These are targets, not real-time SLAs. A consistent daily check-in covers all of them.

## 5. Tone Rules for Community Response

Community replies inherit the four Voice Pillars from LDO002 Section 4, with one adjustment: **Direct Warmth leads** in conversation, more than it does in a caption, because a reply is addressed to one specific person, not a general audience.

| Situation | ✓ On-voice reply | ✗ Off-voice reply |
|---|---|---|
| Compliment | "Thank you — this means a lot." | "OMG thank you so much!! 🥹💕💕" |
| Simple product question | "The Lip Fix runs true to shade. Happy to help if you have a specific one in mind." | "Great question babe!! Let me know if u need anything else 😊" |
| Shipping/order question | "Checking on this now — I'll follow up here shortly." | "So sorry for the wait!! We're working SO hard on this 🙏" |
| Disagreement or criticism | "That's fair feedback — thank you for saying it directly." | Silence, or a defensive correction |

**The test remains the same as LDO002 Section 4:** read the reply aloud. If it sounds like it's trying to win the person over rather than simply answer them, rewrite it.

**Forbidden language (Appendix C) applies in replies exactly as it applies in captions.** A reply written quickly under real-time pressure is not exempt.

## 6. Comment & Message Triage

Every incoming comment or DM is sorted into one of five categories before a reply is written.

**1. Simple and positive** — compliments, general enthusiasm. Reply warmly, briefly, per Section 5.

**2. Product question** — shade match, ingredients, how-to-use. Reply with fact, per LDO002 Pillar B register. If the answer touches a Tier 3 claim under LDO006 Section 6, do not improvise a performance claim — refer to what's already published or say the answer will follow.

**3. Logistics** — shipping, order status, returns. Reply with a concrete next step, not a general apology.

**4. Complaint or negative experience** — a real customer with a real problem. Acknowledge without defensiveness, move the conversation to DM if it involves order details, and do not promise a resolution you can't confirm. If the complaint concerns a safety or reaction issue with a product, this is escalated immediately — see Section 8.

**5. Not a genuine engagement** — spam, bait, or a comment clearly designed to provoke rather than communicate. Do not reply. See Section 7.

## 7. The Do-Not-Engage List

Some messages are better left unanswered. Responding can escalate what silence would have let pass.

- **Bait and provocation** — comments designed to produce a reactive or defensive reply for someone else's entertainment or content.
- **Requests to state or confirm forbidden vocabulary** (Appendix C) — do not repeat the word back, even to correct it.
- **Medical or dermatological diagnosis requests** — "will this help my [condition]" — this is a Responsible Person question (LDO006 Section 15), not a community-reply question. A generic, non-diagnostic reply is fine; a specific medical claim is not.
- **Anything that would require inventing a product claim in real time** — if you don't already know the answer, don't guess in public.

Not replying is a decision, not a failure to respond. It should never be confused with ignoring a genuine complaint (Section 6, category 4), which always gets a reply.

## 8. Escalation to Crisis Communication

Most days, nothing here is needed. This section exists for the days something is.

**Escalate immediately to LDO008 if:**
- A single piece of content receives an unusual volume of negative comments in a short window.
- A complaint involves a physical reaction, safety concern, or allegation the product caused harm.
- A comment thread starts spreading to other accounts or being screenshotted.
- Anyone identifiable as press, an influencer with meaningful reach, or a competitor account engages negatively.
- You are unsure whether something is routine — when in doubt, escalate; LDO008 Section 3 has a fast severity check.

**Until Crisis Communication is invoked, do not:** delete comments, argue publicly, or issue an apology that hasn't been thought through. LDO008 Section 5 (First Hour Protocol) governs what happens next.

## 9. Language & Localization

Replies follow the same EN/ES split established in LDO002: reply in the language the person wrote in. Where a comment thread is mixed-language, default to the language of the original post.

Tone rules in Section 5 apply identically in both languages — a Spanish reply should feel exactly as warm and exactly as restrained as its English equivalent, not softer or stiffer in either direction.

## 10. The Learning Loop

A question asked more than twice in a month is not a community-management problem anymore — it's a content gap. When a pattern appears:

- Note it, however briefly.
- At the next content-planning point (LDO004's monthly review, Section 8), feed it back as a candidate for a future post — most often Pillar B (material honesty) content, since repeated questions are usually about ingredients, shade, or use.

This keeps community response from being a one-way cost center and turns it into an input for the content strategy it sits beside.
