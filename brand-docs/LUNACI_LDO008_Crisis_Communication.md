## 1. Purpose and Boundary

A crisis document written during a crisis is not a plan — it is a panic. This document exists so that if LUNACI faces a negative viral moment, a competitor attack, or an internal content mistake, the response follows a decision that was already made calmly, in advance, rather than one improvised under pressure.

It governs the period between "something has gone wrong" and "the situation is stable enough to return to LDO007's routine operations." It does not replace LDO007 (routine community response), LDO006 (AI/legal governance), or LDO004 (content strategy) — it activates when any of those alone is insufficient.

## 3. Severity Classification

Every escalation from LDO007 Section 8 is classified before any response is drafted. Classification determines everything that follows.

**Level 1 — Contained**
A negative comment thread, a single dissatisfied customer, or a minor factual correction needed. Confined to one post or one conversation. No safety implication.
*Response owner:* Farshad Zamani, using Section 8 playbooks directly. No formal activation of this document beyond following its playbook.

**Level 2 — Spreading**
Negative sentiment moving across multiple posts or accounts, a complaint involving a physical reaction to a product, or engagement from an account with meaningful reach (press, competitor, influencer). Not yet public-narrative territory, but capable of becoming so within hours.
*Response owner:* Farshad Zamani as both AI Governance Owner and Responsible Person (LDO006 Section 15) — a Level 2 event frequently touches both brand tone and product-claim questions at once.

**Level 3 — Public Narrative**
The situation is being discussed outside LUNACI's own channels — press coverage, a widely shared post, a safety allegation gaining traction.

Classification is not permanent — a Level 1 issue can become Level 2 within an hour. Reclassify at the start of every check-in (Section 4). **Severity level and the need to contact counsel are related but not identical** — see Section 6, which sets its own trigger conditions independent of the level number.

## 4. The First Hour Protocol

The first sixty minutes after an escalation matter more than any single word in the eventual response. Four steps, in order, before a full response is published:

**1. Pause, don't publish a full response.** No detailed public reply, deletion, or final statement is issued in the first hour, except where Section 8's specific playbook says otherwise (e.g., an active safety complaint always gets an immediate private acknowledgment — see Playbook D). Where the situation is visible enough that saying nothing at all would itself look like a failure to respond, Section 5's holding statement — not a full response — may be used within this hour.

**2. Classify.** Assign a severity level (Section 3) based on what is known right now, not what might happen.

**3. Gather facts, not opinions.** What was actually said, by whom, where, and — if it concerns a product — what SKU, what batch, what claim. Do not speculate about intent or motive of the person involved. Begin the incident log (Section 11) at this step, not after the situation resolves.

**4. Decide the channel before the words.** Public reply, private DM, no response at all, or an on-platform statement — decided using Section 9's decision tree, before a single sentence of a full response is drafted.

Only after these four steps does drafting of a full, final response begin.

## 5. Public Holding Statement Template

"Pause, don't publish" protects against a rushed, wrong response — but silence has a cost too, once a situation is visible enough that people are waiting for LUNACI to say something. A holding statement fills that gap: it says LUNACI has seen the situation and is handling it, without committing to specifics that haven't been verified yet.

**When to use it:** any Level 2 or Level 3 situation where visibility is high enough that continued silence would itself become the story. Never required for Level 1 — a Level 1 issue is handled through LDO007's routine tone (LDO002 Section 4), not a formal statement.

### Template (English)

> We're aware of this and are looking into it directly. We'll share a further update here by [specific time or date — never "soon"]. If you have direct concerns about a LUNACI product, please reach us at [contact channel] so we can follow up properly.

### Plantilla (Español)

> Somos conscientes de esta situación y la estamos revisando directamente. Compartiremos más información aquí antes de [hora o fecha específica — nunca "pronto"]. Si tiene alguna inquietud directa sobre un producto LUNACI, contáctenos en [canal de contacto] para poder darle seguimiento adecuado.

### Rules for Any Holding Statement
- **Always names a specific follow-up time**, not a vague one — "by 6pm today" or "by tomorrow," never "soon" or "shortly."
- **Never admits fault and never denies fault.** A holding statement acknowledges that something is being looked into; it does not conclude anything.
- **Never states a product claim, safety conclusion, or root cause** — that belongs in the full response once facts are confirmed (Section 4, step 3).
- **Follows Respectful Directness (LDO002 Section 4)** exactly as any other LUNACI communication does — no urgency language, no over-apologizing, no exclamation marks.
- Once posted, **the named follow-up time is binding.** If the full response isn't ready by then, a brief update extending the timeline is posted rather than letting the deadline pass silently.

## 6. Counsel Notification Triggers

The prior revision of this document tied counsel contact only to Level 3. That was too coarse: some Level 2 events carry legal exposure well before a situation becomes public narrative. Counsel is contacted whenever **any** of the following is true, regardless of severity level:

- The complaint alleges a physical reaction, injury, or health effect from a LUNACI product (this always applies from Playbook D, even at first contact).
- The person raising the issue mentions, threatens, or implies legal action, a regulatory complaint, or a consumer-protection authority.
- A journalist, press outlet, or media contact reaches out directly, in any form.
- The situation touches a claim governed by LDO006 Section 6 (Cosmetic Claims) where the accuracy of a past claim is being challenged, not just its tone.
- The event is classified Level 3 for any reason.

**This is a checklist, not a judgment call.** If any line is true, counsel is notified the same day the trigger is identified — waiting for the situation to "become" Level 3 is exactly the delay this section exists to prevent.

## 7. Response Principles

Every crisis response, regardless of scenario, follows the same three principles — a direct extension of the brand's own voice under pressure, not a departure from it.

**Dignity, not defensiveness.** Vaqar does not disappear under criticism. A response that argues, minimizes, or performs outrage on the brand's behalf contradicts the brand more than the original criticism did.

**Say only what is confirmed.** Nothing is stated publicly that isn't verified. A holding statement (Section 5) is always safer than a guess that later needs correcting.

**Respectful Directness, especially here.** LDO002's fourth Voice Pillar — never manipulate, exaggerate, or manufacture urgency — applies with more force during a crisis, not less. No response should try to rush the audience past the issue.

## 8. Scenario Playbooks

### Playbook A — Negative Viral Content
A piece of LUNACI's own content is receiving significant negative reaction (not a product complaint — a reaction to the content itself: tone, imagery, a perceived misstep).

- Do not delete the post in the first hour unless it violates a platform policy itself.
- Assess honestly: is the criticism fair? If yes, a brief, direct acknowledgment beats silence. If the criticism is based on a misunderstanding, one clear clarifying reply, once — not repeated defense in every comment.
- No countdown, no "we hear you and are taking action" without an actual action to name.

### Playbook B — Competitor Attack or Disparagement
A competitor or affiliated account is making negative claims about LUNACI publicly.

- Do not respond to the competitor directly or by name. Appendix C's forbidden-language and tone standards apply even more strictly toward competitors than toward customers.
- If the claim is factually false in a way that matters (not just unflattering), a single factual, calm correction on LUNACI's own channel — never a reply on theirs.
- This is escalated to Level 2 by default, since it involves reputational judgment outside routine response.

### Playbook C — Internal Content Mistake
Something LUNACI published violates its own governance — forbidden language slipped through (Appendix C), an AI disclosure was missed (LDO006 Section 5), or a claim exceeded what a product can support (LDO006 Section 6).

- Correct the asset first (edit, remove, or add the missing disclosure), then decide whether a public acknowledgment is warranted.
- A quiet correction is appropriate for a minor, low-visibility slip. A visible acknowledgment is warranted if the content had meaningful reach or if the error touches a legal disclosure requirement — in that case, do not skip the acknowledgment for the sake of looking clean.
- Log the failure against the relevant document's validation gate (LDO001, LDO006 Section 11) so the same mistake doesn't recur.

### Playbook D — Product Safety or Reaction Complaint
A customer reports a skin reaction, an allergic response, or any safety-adjacent issue.

- **This playbook overrides the "pause, don't publish" default in Section 4.** Acknowledge privately (DM) within the hour, regardless of severity classification — a safety complaint is never left waiting for a full assessment.
- Move immediately to private channel; do not discuss product safety specifics in public comments.
- This is automatically Level 2 minimum, and automatically triggers counsel notification per Section 6.
- Request the details needed for the Product Information File equivalent: which SKU, batch if known, how the product was used, nature of the reaction.

## 9. The Decision Tree

Before drafting any full response:

```
Is there a safety or health claim involved?
  → YES: Playbook D. Private acknowledgment within the hour, regardless of level.
          Counsel notified same day (Section 6).
  → NO: continue.

Is the criticism directed at LUNACI's own published content?
  → YES: Playbook A.
  → NO: continue.

Is it coming from a competitor or clearly competitive source?
  → YES: Playbook B.
  → NO: continue.

Did LUNACI make an actual governance or claims error?
  → YES: Playbook C.
  → NO: This is likely a Level 1, routine LDO007 matter —
         return to Community Operations.

At every branch: does this trip any Section 6 counsel trigger?
  → YES: notify counsel the same day, independent of playbook chosen.
```

## 10. Approval Authority

**Current state:** Farshad Zamani approves every crisis response at every level, as both AI Governance Owner and Responsible Person (LDO006 Section 15). There is no second-approver requirement at this stage of the brand, unlike the two-reviewer pattern used elsewhere (LDO001, LMB004) — a crisis response cannot wait for a second person who doesn't yet exist in the organization.

**When a second person is added** to community or brand management, this section is updated: Level 1 responses may be approved by the community response owner alone; Level 2 and Level 3 require Farshad Zamani's direct approval before anything is published.

## 11. Incident Log & Closure Artifacts

Every escalation that reaches this document — Level 1 or higher — gets a logged record. Memory is not a substitute for a written incident log, even for a small event; the pattern across several small events is often the real signal (see Section 12).

### Incident Log Template

```
Incident ID:            CRISIS-[YYYY-MM]-[sequence]   e.g. CRISIS-2026-11-01
Detected:                [date + time]
Source / channel:        [Instagram / TikTok / Pinterest / LinkedIn / DM / email / other]
Escalated from:          LDO007 trigger (state which — Section 8 of LDO007)

TIMESTAMPED FIRST HOUR LOG
Pause confirmed at:      [timestamp]
Severity classified at:  [timestamp] → Level [1/2/3]
Facts gathered at:       [timestamp] — summary: [ ]
Channel decision at:     [timestamp] → [public / private / holding statement / no response]
Holding statement posted at: [timestamp, or "not used"]

Playbook applied:        A / B / C / D
Counsel trigger check:   [Section 6 line(s) matched, or "none"]
Counsel notified at:     [timestamp, or "not applicable"]
Full response published at: [timestamp]
Approved by:             Farshad Zamani

STATUS: Open / Monitoring / Closed
Closed at:               [timestamp]
```

### Closure Checklist
An incident may be marked **Closed** only when every line below is checked:

- [ ] No new negative signal for 48 hours (or, for Level 3, the situation has left public discussion)
- [ ] Full response (if one was warranted) has been published and matches what the holding statement promised
- [ ] Any counsel trigger identified in Section 6 has been formally resolved with counsel, not just "sent"
- [ ] Incident log is complete with all timestamps filled in, not left blank retroactively
- [ ] Post-Crisis Review (Section 12) has been completed and any resulting document updates identified

An incident with any unchecked box remains **Open** or **Monitoring**, regardless of how much time has passed.

---

## 12. Post-Crisis Review

Once the Closure Checklist (Section 11) is satisfied, a short review closes the incident formally, using the same audit discipline as LDO004 Section 10:

- What severity level was assigned, and was it accurate in hindsight?
- Which playbook was used, and did it fit?
- Was a holding statement used, and did the follow-up meet its own deadline?
- Did any Section 6 counsel trigger fire correctly, or was one missed?
- What would have prevented the issue reaching escalation at all (governance gap, content gap, community-response gap)?
- Does any governing document (LDO001–LDO008, LMB series) need a specific update as a result?

This review is written into the incident log (Section 11), however briefly, even for a Level 1 event that never truly escalated.

## 13. Governing Authority & Precedence

*Effective under Revision 1.1, superseding Revision 1.0. Reviewed alongside LDO007 at the end of each two-month phase, and immediately after any Level 2 or Level 3 event.*

LDO008 is subordinate to, and may never override:
- Brand Constitution
- Appendix A — Canonical Definitions & Brand Invariants (LMB003)
- Appendix B — Brand Constants
- Appendix C — Approved & Forbidden Language
- LMB001, LMB002, LMB003, LMB004
- LDO006 — AI Generation Governance (Responsible Person authority on any product-safety or claims matter)
- LDO007 — Community Operations (source of escalation into this document)

Where an event touches product safety or a legal claim, LDO006's Responsible Person role — not this document — holds final authority on what may be stated publicly about the product itself. This document governs the communication process and its operational discipline; it does not override product-compliance judgment.
